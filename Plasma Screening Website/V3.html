Yukawa How the Yukawa / Debye-Hückel simulation works ▾
Potential
V(r) = A · exp(−κr) / r
This is the Debye-Hückel screened Coulomb potential. The exponential factor exp(−κr) causes the interaction to decay much faster than bare Coulomb (1/r), representing the shielding effect of the surrounding electron cloud.
Parameters
κ= 1/λ_DScreening wavenumber [λ_D⁻¹]
A= e²/4πε₀k_BT·λ_DNormalized Yukawa prefactor [dimensionless]
cutoff= 3 λ_DPair cutoff — V(3λ_D) ≈ 0.05·V(0) [λ_D]
Integration
Velocity Verlet algorithm with timestep dt = 0.001 τ. A velocity-rescaling NVT thermostat maintains constant temperature by rescaling all velocities every 20 steps to match the target k_BT.
Electrons
Electrons are implicit — they do not appear as particles. Their entire effect is encoded in the screening parameter κ = 1/λ_D. This is exact within Debye-Hückel theory for weakly coupled plasmas (Γ ≪ 1). The analytical electron density around each proton is n_e(r)/n₀ = 1 + A·exp(−κr)/r, shown as the blue glow halo on screen.
Validity
Valid for weakly coupled plasmas where Γ ≪ 1 — thermal energy dominates over electrostatic potential energy. Your current Γ is shown in the stats bar above.

WCA Core
The Weeks-Chandler-Andersen potential V_WCA(r) = 4ε[(σ/r)¹²−(σ/r)⁶]+ε for r < 2^(1/6)σ, else 0. This is purely repulsive — it mimics the quantum zero-point pressure and Pauli exclusion principle that prevents electrons from collapsing onto protons in a classical simulation. σ values: p-p = 0.30 λ_D, e-p = 0.05 λ_D, e-e = 0.04 λ_D.
Mass Ratio
Electron mass = m_p / 100 (reduced from physical 1/1836). The full ratio requires dt ≈ 1×10⁻⁶ τ — with 10,000 steps that simulates only 0.01 τ of real dynamics. At 1:100 a stable dt = 0.0001 τ gives 1 τ of meaningful dynamics. The Debye screening statistics are unchanged by this approximation.
Integration
Velocity Verlet with dt = 0.0001 τ (10× smaller than Yukawa — needed for fast electrons). Separate NVT thermostats for protons and electrons because at the same temperature, electrons (lighter) have much higher velocities and require tighter coupling.
What you see
Red dots = protons. Purple dots = electrons. Purple trails show recent electron paths. The Debye cloud forms organically — electrons cluster near protons due to Coulomb attraction, visible both in the particle positions and in the e–p RDF peak above g = 1.
Theory About plasma screening and Debye-Hückel theory ▾
What is plasma screening?
In a plasma, free charges rearrange themselves to reduce the effective electric field of any given ion. A positive ion (proton) attracts nearby electrons, forming a negatively charged cloud that partially cancels the proton's field. Beyond a characteristic distance — the Debye length — the proton appears electrically neutral to other particles. This is called Debye screening or electrostatic shielding.
The Debye Length λ_D
λ_D = √(ε₀k_BT / n_e·e²)
λ_D is the e-folding length of the screened potential — at r = λ_D, the Yukawa potential has decayed to 1/e ≈ 37% of its unscreened value. Higher density or lower temperature → shorter λ_D → stronger screening. The simulation box is always 10 λ_D per side so you see many screening lengths.
The Coupling Parameter Γ
Γ = e²/4πε₀k_BT·r_WS
Γ is the ratio of electrostatic potential energy to thermal energy at the Wigner-Seitz radius r_WS = (3/4πn)^(1/3). Γ ≪ 1 → weakly coupled (thermal energy dominates, Debye-Hückel valid). Γ ≫ 1 → strongly coupled (potential energy dominates, liquid/crystal-like order). Your plasma conditions are shown in the stats bar.
Debye-Hückel Theory
Derived by Peter Debye and Erich Hückel in 1923, the theory solves the linearized Poisson-Boltzmann equation to find the electrostatic potential around a test charge in a plasma:
φ(r) = (Ze/4πε₀) · exp(−r/λ_D) / r
The electron density around a proton follows:
n_e(r)/n₀ = 1 + A · exp(−κr) / r
This is exact in the weakly coupled limit (Γ → 0). The Yukawa simulation directly implements this screened potential for the proton-proton interaction.
Radial Distribution Function g(r)
g(r) measures the probability of finding a particle at distance r from a reference particle, relative to a purely random (ideal gas) distribution. g = 1 everywhere means no correlations — a perfect gas. In a screened plasma: the Yukawa RDF shows g < 1 at small r (protons avoid each other), while the Coulomb e–p RDF shows g > 1 at small r (electrons cluster near protons — the Debye cloud).
Applications
Plasma screening is fundamental to stellar interiors, fusion plasmas, semiconductor doping, electrolyte chemistry, and colloid science. The Debye length in the solar core is ~10⁻¹¹ m; in a semiconductor it is ~10⁻⁸ m; in seawater ~1 nm.
